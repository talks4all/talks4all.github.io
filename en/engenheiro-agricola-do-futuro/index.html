UNIPAMPA, Agricultural Engineering programme

The Agricultural Engineer of the future

The programme, the field and artificial intelligence.

When
27 October 2026, 6:30 pm
Where
UNIPAMPA, Alegrete campus
Format
Talk built around five questions put to the room, with a clip, reels and songs made with AI
Audience
Students and faculty of the Agricultural Engineering programme

About this talk

The five versions of the Agricultural Engineering curriculum add up to 1,846 pages. The phrase "artificial intelligence" appears zero times. The talk starts from that number, found by an AI that read all five PDFs and built a programme website in an afternoon, with every figure checked against the original document.

In the middle, the field: drones, image-based spraying, AI-driven irrigation, barns that listen, silos with sensors and satellite maps, with real cases from Brazil and from UNIPAMPA itself. And also where the model is wrong while looking right, from 99% in the lab to 31% outside it.

At the end, education: what AI changes in how students learn and are assessed, and an exploratory proposal, for discussion, of a curriculum for those starting in 2027. The golden rule runs through all of it: the model recommends, the engineer signs.

Running order

  1. The programme: the curriculum explained in a website

    Curriculum with a prerequisite map, schedule planner and five PDFs compared on one page. AI read and built it, and every figure was checked against the source.

  2. AI today: why one classmate succeeds and another does not

    The request, the model, the tool, the material and the conversation. A weak prompt returns generic ranges; a good one sizes the pump for 50 ha of rice.

  3. Same question, three models

    Which Brazilian ministry ordinance regulates pesticide application by drones? All three get the number right and disagree on the requirements. Convincing is not correct.

  4. The field: four ways AI arrives

    Vision, prediction, autonomy and language. Six technologies already in use in Brazil, one per area of the programme, and what each changes for the agricultural engineer.

  5. Real examples

    See & Spray, Solix, Arbus, Smart Irriga, SoundTalks, soybean grading at Embrapa, SORTEX, MapBiomas, Cromai and UNIPAMPA's work on annoni grass and cattle ticks.

  6. The engineer's value shifts

    Routine calculations and standard reports get cheap. Collecting good data, integrating systems, validating the model and signing for it get expensive.

  7. Wrong while looking right

    The model trained outside the Pampa, the neural network that collapses outside the lab, and the invented case law that ended in a fine in a Brazilian court.

  8. Education: a private tutor, not a cheat sheet

    First semester students delivering in two hours what used to be a final year project. Delivery got cheap, judgement got expensive, and assessment has to follow.

  9. Redesign: a curriculum for those starting in 2027

    Exploratory proposal: a data and AI track, one real problem per semester, four levels of AI use in assessment and a yearly review.

  10. Five habits and one commitment for each side of the room

    Check, have the AI ask and critique, compare two AIs, protect the farmer's data and declare your use. For students and for faculty.

What you take home

  • The model recommends. You are the one who signs.
  • AI does not arrive in the field as a new machine, it arrives inside all of them.
  • Same AI, different results: the difference is in how you use it.
  • An outside model gets the Pampa wrong while looking right. Your advantage is knowing what it does not.
  • Delivery got cheap. Judgement got expensive.
  • Use AI to go beyond what you would do alone, not to skip the effort.
