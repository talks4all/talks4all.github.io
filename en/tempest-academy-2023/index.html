Tempest Academy Conference 2023

ML, AutoML, XAI and AML in Android malware detection

Eight tools later, the bottleneck is still the data.

When
2023
Where
Recife, Pernambuco, Brazil
Format
Technical talk on applied research, with an Ask the Experts session at the end
Audience
Security practitioners, researchers and computing students

About this talk

This talk is about applied research and about the road taken: the trajectory, the challenges, the lessons and the insights of applying machine learning, AutoML, explainable AI and adversarial machine learning to the problem of detecting Android malware.

The thread running through it is the AutoML pipeline, from data acquisition to hyperparameter optimisation, with two questions the literature tends to skip: are there domain specific optimisations, and are the existing tools transparent enough for anyone to understand what they did?

Eight systems were built along the way, among them dataset generators, a framework for evaluating feature selection methods, and an AutoML solution with transparency and interpretability. The hardest part, from start to finish, was the data.

In numbers

  • 8 toolsSystems built over the course of the research, from the dataset generator to the AutoML framework with interpretability.
  • 100%Of the 38 papers analysed, not one carries enough information to reproduce the dataset it used or created.
  • 101,975 samplesThe size of MH-100K, with more than 24,500 features, already published and in use by a technical team at Motorola.
  • 85%Of machine learning projects fail, and the most cited causes sit in the quantity, the quality and the real world alignment of the data.

Running order

  1. The typical machine learning process

    Every stage of the pipeline demands deep domain knowledge, the technical skill to collect, clean, analyse and model the data, and time.

  2. AutoML: what it promises to automate

    Cutting the dependency on specialists and automating the whole pipeline, from data acquisition to architecture search. Plus the two questions left out: is there domain specific optimisation, and where is the transparency?

  3. Datasets: what the literature leaves out

    Thirty eight papers analysed across four groups. More than 40% of the datasets restricted or unavailable, almost 60% available but limited or outdated, and not one paper with enough information for reproduction.

  4. Building your own dataset

    ADBuilder, AMGenerator and AMExplorer: downloading from AndroZoo, extraction with Androguard, labelling through VirusTotal, and the cache problem that hands back an old label as if it were fresh.

  5. When the label goes stale

    Two apps classified as benign in 2019 and 2020 were flagged as malware by more than 18 scanners in 2022. Labelling needs reanalysis, and reanalysis does not scale for free.

  6. MH-100K and MH-1M

    A hundred and one thousand samples and more than 24,500 features already published, with the generation of over a million running for more than five months, capped by the VirusTotal reanalysis rate.

  7. Synthetic data with generative AI

    DroidAugmentor trains and tunes cGANs to augment and vary datasets, with similarity and applicability metrics. AutoDroid scales that execution across container based microservices.

  8. Preprocessing and feature selection

    Where general purpose tools break in this domain, and FS3E, which evaluates nine feature selection methods systematically and reproducibly, on balanced and imbalanced datasets.

  9. Models, hyperparameters and compute cost

    How models behave empirically as the dataset grows, why Extra Trees came out ahead at a lower cost, and the AutoGluon case, which does well on fixed hyperparameters.

  10. Transparency and interpretability

    AutoML as a black box, what MH-AutoML exposes of what happened inside it, and what LIME and SHAP show when the same study runs across several datasets.

  11. Label flipping attacks

    Random, entropy based and silhouette based label flipping. Which variant hurts most, which models hold up better, and which datasets prove less susceptible.

  12. The end, or the start of the middle

    Closing thoughts: there are challenges at every stage of the pipeline, and without transparency and interpretability you cannot even compare the solutions against each other.

What you take home

  • Investigate a dataset before trusting it. Much of what is published in this field is unavailable, outdated or not representative.
  • Labels go stale. What was benign in 2019 can be malware to dozens of scanners three years later.
  • General purpose AutoML tools fail in specific domains. Preprocessing and feature selection need techniques from the domain itself.
  • Transparency and interpretability are not decoration. Without them you cannot understand the result or compare AutoML solutions.

Where to go next

  • AndroZoo

    A repository of more than 19 million APKs, the download source used by ADBuilder.

  • VirusTotal

    More than 60 scanners. It is where the labels come from, and also the main bottleneck when generating the datasets.

  • Androguard

    Static feature extraction from the apps, one of the most complete tools of its kind.

  • SHAP

    The explanation framework used in the interpretability case study, across multiple datasets.

  • LIME

    Model agnostic local explanations, the other framework in the case study.

  • AutoGluon

    The AutoML tool behind the fixed hyperparameter case, which performs well even without runtime optimisation.
