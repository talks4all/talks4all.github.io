Pint of Science 2026

AI is going to end your profession

Or not. That part is up to you.

When
May 2026
Where
Alegrete, Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil
Format
Science outreach talk, around 30 minutes, followed by questions from the room
Audience
Open to everyone, no technical background required

About this talk

Pint of Science takes science to the pub. This talk takes to the counter the question nobody can postpone any longer: what does artificial intelligence do to your profession over the next five years.

The route starts on the timeline from 2022 to 2025, goes through what AI really is and what it definitely is not, measures the multiplier effect with a real case from inside UNIPAMPA, and ends on what stays human: judging, deciding and signing your name to it.

None of it requires knowing how to code. The lens is the western frontier of Rio Grande do Sul: farming that already flies drones and reads satellites, industry that already runs computer vision, and a university still teaching the way it did in the twentieth century.

In numbers

  • 128xThe productivity gap between six people working seven days and one student with AI working two hours, on the same problem.
  • 5 daysHow long ChatGPT took to reach one million users, in late 2022.
  • 97 millionNew jobs projected by the World Economic Forum, against 85 million that stop existing.
  • R$ 29 millionWhat iFood paid for a marketing startup built with AI by a young man from the countryside of Minas Gerais.

Running order

  1. The question is not if, it is how and when

    From 2022 to 2025: ChatGPT reaches a million users in five days, AI passes the bar exam and the college entrance exam, writes code faster than any human, and starts deciding in real time out in the field.

  2. What AI is, and what it is not

    It has read almost everything ever published, never forgets and never takes a holiday. It also hallucinates with total confidence, has no real common sense, and is not the humanoid robot from the movies.

  3. From the library to the super tutor

    Three eras of access to knowledge: analogue until 2000, digital until 2022, and the current one, where the best tutor in the world has infinite patience and costs nothing.

  4. The multiplier effect

    Six students in seven days against one student with AI in two hours. Plus three applications delivered in the first semester of an engineering degree that would have been final year projects five years ago.

  5. The job market in three bands

    High risk over the next five years, deep transformation, and the creation of fields that did not exist. Where each profession lands, and why.

  6. Farming and industry around here

    Drones, satellites, soil sensors and smart irrigation in the fields. Computer vision in the meat plant and optical sorting in rice processing.

  7. The nineteenth century university

    Twenty first century students, taught by twentieth century teachers, in nineteenth century institutions, for an eighteenth century system. What has to change, and what has already changed in practice.

  8. The human domain

    Critical thinking, ethics and judgement, empathy, contextual creativity and interdisciplinary collaboration. The skills you cannot pick up in a three hour course.

  9. Three steps to start

    What to do today, for free. What to do this year, on a small budget. And what to build for the long run, once the tool stops being the advantage.

What you take home

  • AI does not level the playing field. It widens the gap between those who use it well and those who do not.
  • No profession disappears whole. What changes hands is the task, and the repetitive ones go first.
  • Producing a first draft is no longer the differentiator. Choosing, verifying and owning the decision still are.
  • The best time to prepare was five years ago. The second best time is now.
