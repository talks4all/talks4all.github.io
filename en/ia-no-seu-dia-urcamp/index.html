AI is already in your day

The question is who is in charge.

When
14 September 2026, 7:30 pm
Where
URCAMP, Alegrete campus, room 306
Format
Roughly one hour, built around six questions put to the room
Audience
Students and faculty of the Business Administration programme
Host
Prof. Rafael Augusto Dill

About this talk

Everyone in the room used artificial intelligence in the last 24 hours. Not everyone knew it. The talk starts there: with what already happens unnoticed, from the feed that picked what you saw today to the résumé a piece of software read before any person did.

The lens is management: finance, people, marketing and sales, operations and strategy. Notice that none of those lines says decide, and that verb is exactly the one that stays with the manager.

The second half is about the university. Change in the job market starts four years earlier, inside the classroom, and it has already happened one course down the hall: first semester engineering students delivering in two hours what would have been a final year project five years ago.

In numbers

  • 6 questionsThe talk is built around six questions put directly to the audience, from the first slide to the last.
  • 0 lines of codeNo part of the content requires knowing how to program.
  • Article 20The clause of the Brazilian data protection law that lets a citizen ask for review of a decision made by a machine.
  • 15 minutes a dayThe habit that, kept for thirty days, changes more than a forty hour course.

Running order

  1. Who used AI in the last 24 hours

    Every hand goes up. The list of what you noticed, and the much longer list of what you did not.

  2. What it is, and what it is not

    Software that learns patterns from data, costs cents per task, and cannot be held responsible for anything it does.

  3. The shift: from coding rules to showing examples

    Where fixed rules break, from the fraud filter that blocks a customer on holiday to the credit cutoff that rejects a reliable payer. And where learning from data wins.

  4. Where it shines, and where it fails badly

    It invents sources, reproduces bias and does not know when it does not know. Read that column again thinking about people: the description fits both of us.

  5. The measure is not the task, it is the cost of the error

    What decides whether to automate is not how hard the task is, it is what happens when the answer is wrong.

  6. What changes in each management function

    Finance, people, marketing and sales, operations and strategy. What the tool does, and what still requires a human signature.

  7. The western frontier, in practice

    Farming that is already a heavy AI user, local retail that wins on the basics, and public administration that needs scale without hiring.

  8. Professions do not vanish, tasks change hands

    Every profession is a bundle of tasks, and AI takes the textual, repetitive ones first. Redesign your own bundle and you stay in the game.

  9. The quiet risk: outsourcing your thinking

    Using without reading is the new copy and paste. If you cannot do it, you cannot check it, and the average answer becomes the ceiling for the whole team.

  10. Change starts at the university

    Three applications delivered in two hours by first semester engineering students, and what the course itself had to become for that to happen.

  11. Four habits to start tomorrow morning

    Give context, ask for three versions, verify anything with a number or a source, and never hand in what you could not defend on your own.

What you take home

  • AI stopped being an IT topic. It became decision infrastructure.
  • The measure for automating is not how hard the task is. It is the cost of the error.
  • What now counts on a CV: asking well, verifying, knowing the context, deciding on incomplete data, and signing your name to it.
  • Use AI to think more, never to think less.
